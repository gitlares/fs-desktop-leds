Why this exists

“I bought the desk a year ago.
The lights still needed my phone.”

I’m not a gamer. I just wanted to make my workspace a little nicer. My desk came with LED lights, but changing them meant reaching for my phone.

So I built a small Mac app to put that control where I was already working. A warm color in the evening. A little movement with a song. Something nice at the edge of an ordinary workday.

FS stands for Fast and Simple. That’s the idea I want to keep.

Before you download

A small beta.
Clear expectations.

Built for a specific, useful job. Help us find out which desks and strips it works with.

Will it work with my lights?

The tested controller is ELK-BLEDOM, using the alternate protocol. Similar names don’t guarantee the same hardware. Other controllers and individual LED addressing are unverified. Current effects change the whole strip together.

What does my Mac need?

An Apple Silicon Mac running macOS 14 or later, with Bluetooth. This first download is for Apple Silicon only. Close the phone’s lighting app if it is holding the Bluetooth connection.

How do I get started?

Open the downloaded disk image, drag Desktop LEDs into Applications, then open it. Click the lightbulb in the menu bar and choose Settings → Lights → Search for lights. Connect your strip and choose a color.

Why does it ask for permissions?

Bluetooth controls the strip. Ambient and Mac audio need screen and system-audio capture permission. Microphone access is only needed if you choose that source. Notification flashes need Accessibility. Capture samples are processed locally and discarded.

Updates and telemetry?

There is no telemetry or analytics. Choose Check for Updates… to check for a signed update with Sparkle. Automatic checks and downloads are off, and system-profile reporting is disabled. Update requests go to GitHub; your lighting, screen and audio data stay on your Mac.

How lightweight is it?

The app is native Swift, with no bundled browser or language runtime. Solid colors stop the animation timer when they settle. Media capture runs only in the modes that need it, with small screen samples and bounded processing.

Memory varies by mode and macOS version. See the measured footprint and test conditions.

What should I expect from the beta?

Audio response needs more real-world testing. Notification detection depends on macOS’s visible banners and can miss alerts; it does not see notices hidden by Focus. A normal quit requests lights off. Force quit cannot do that.
